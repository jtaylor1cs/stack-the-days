Stack the Days is a simple bet: if I log a real skill-building session every
day and stack them end to end, a year from now there's a trail of actual
progress instead of a vague memory of "I meant to learn that."

## The project: electric guitar, from zero

I've never played guitar. No childhood lessons, no dusty acoustic in a
closet, no chords I half-remember. That's deliberate — starting from true
zero makes the curve honest and the logs useful to anyone else starting
from the same place.

This is an **ultralearning** project in the Scott Young sense: a short,
intense, self-directed push on one skill, with a plan instead of vibes.
Three things I'm committing to up front:

1. **Daily reps, even short ones.** Consistency beats marathon weekend
   sessions. A rough plan: short focused practice on weekdays, longer
   sessions when time allows.
2. **Direct practice over passive theory.** Less "watch ten videos about
   music theory," more "play the thing badly, then better."
3. **Log it the same day.** Not a polished retrospective a week later —
   what actually happened, while it's still fresh, including the parts
   that didn't work.

## What the daily posts will look like

Every entry follows the same shape on purpose, so a year of them stays
scannable: what I practiced, how long, what clicked, what didn't, and
tomorrow's focus. No filler, no "amazing progress today!" padding — just
the log.

## Why public, why comments

Writing for an audience — even a small one — is a forcing function against
half-effort days. And if you're further along than me, the comments are
open: corrections, better drills, and "stop doing that, here's why" are
all welcome. This is the first post; the first real practice log follows
next.
